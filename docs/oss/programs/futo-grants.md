# FUTO Grants

Status: CURRENT (Last verified: 2026-08-21)

## Program

Privately funded grantmaker backing open source projects and people "working to challenge the tech oligopoly." Two tracks: Legendary Grants for established projects and nonprofits, and Microgrants for small or early-stage projects. The grants page states "Applications are now open." Past legendary grantees include Signal, Tor Project, Ladybird, CalyxOS, NetBSD and Haiku; past microgrant recipients include KiCad, VLC, Blender, FFmpeg, GIMP, KeePassXC and Asahi Linux.

## Official URL

https://www.futo.org/grants/

## What it funds

Open source projects aligned with FUTO's anti-big-tech, user-sovereignty thesis. No published thematic call structure; the selection appears to be judgement-based rather than criteria-based.

## Eligibility

No formal eligibility criteria are published on the grants page. Observed pattern:

- Legendary Grants are "generally reserved for established entities we trust," with an ongoing relationship maintained afterwards.
- Microgrants target small or early-stage projects, but FUTO states "Many of our microgrants are unsolicited: Our employees identify projects they personally recommend and we place a donation in FUTO's name." This means the realistic path is being noticed, not applying.

## Typical funding

- Legendary Grants: over $5 million awarded across roughly 20 projects. No per-grant range published.
- Microgrants: $1,000-$5,000, one-time. Over $60,000 awarded across 52 projects.

## Application requirements

Email grantapps@futo.org. No form, no published template, no stated review timeline or criteria. Assume a short, direct, technically credible email is the expected format.

## Sill fit

LOW now, MEDIUM later for a microgrant only. Legendary Grants are out of reach by definition. The microgrant track is the only plausible one and it is dominated by staff nomination of projects that already exist and are already used — every named microgrant recipient is a mature, widely-installed tool. A pre-alpha scaffold with zero releases has nothing for a FUTO employee to have personally used and recommended. On thesis fit, a provider-neutral local-first terminal that avoids lock-in to any single AI vendor is genuinely on-message for FUTO, which is the one point in Sill's favour here.

## Missing evidence

- A working, installable release someone could actually run.
- Any user base at all — microgrants reward tools people already use.
- A clear articulation of the anti-lock-in / user-sovereignty angle (local-first, provider-neutral, no telemetry, works without AI).

## Application strategy

Low cost, low probability, worth one short email once there is a shipped release with real users. Emailing now would burn the only impression available. Because grants are frequently unsolicited, the higher-leverage strategy is visibility within the communities FUTO staff inhabit, not the application itself.
