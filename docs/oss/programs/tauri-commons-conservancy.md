# Tauri ecosystem and The Commons Conservancy

Status: CURRENT — governance/legal home, not a funder (Last verified: 2026-08-21)

## Program

Two linked but distinct things, both often misfiled as funding sources.
The Tauri Programme is governed within The Commons Conservancy, with a Working Group, Board, Domains and Teams. Tauri raises money for itself via Open Collective (fiscally hosted by Open Source Collective) and GitHub Sponsors.
The Commons Conservancy is a Dutch stichting offering shared legal infrastructure — a "virtual not-for-profit" per project — at no cost, so projects do not each have to form their own foundation.

## Official URL

https://tauri.app/about/governance/ and https://commonsconservancy.org/how/

## What it funds

Neither funds downstream applications. Tauri's Open Collective exists to cover Tauri's own infrastructure costs and eventually pay Tauri contributors; there is no regranting program for apps built with Tauri, and the collective's scale makes one implausible (estimated annual budget around $30,000, balance around $109,000 as of 2026-08). The Commons Conservancy explicitly does not handle money directly — donations are routed through a separate specialised charity partner.

## Eligibility

Commons Conservancy: free software / open source projects. Process is: inform yourself, notify intent to join, receive a notice of eligibility after a qualification check, appoint a "quartermaster" while the Conservancy appoints a case officer, define mission and founding statutes conforming to the Conservancy's mission, have programme officials sign the Pledge and Code of Conduct, then statutes are approved and published in the DRACC series. A "Graduation" procedure exists to transfer intangible assets out later if the project forms its own foundation.

## Typical funding

€0 from either. Both are governance and infrastructure, not grantmaking.

## Application requirements

Commons Conservancy: notification of intent, then statute drafting with an assigned case officer, then signatures. No monetary application. Tauri: no grant application exists.

## Sill fit

LOW. The Commons Conservancy solves problems Sill does not have — copyright assignment across many contributors, neutral governance during disputes, trademark stewardship, avoiding foundation setup costs. With one maintainer, no contributors and no assets, adopting a governance framework would be overhead theatre that signals more maturity than exists. Its real value would arrive if Sill ever attracts co-maintainers and needs neutral IP holding.

## Missing evidence

- Multiple contributors with copyright interests to consolidate.
- A trademark worth holding, or a corporate relationship that needs a neutral counterparty.
- Community governance pressure that a solo maintainer cannot resolve informally.

## Application strategy

Ignore for now. The useful takeaway from the Tauri precedent is the pattern, not the money: Tauri combines a Commons Conservancy governance home with Open Source Collective fiscal hosting and GitHub Sponsors. That is a sensible destination architecture for Sill years from now, and it is worth copying only in that order — Sponsors first, fiscal host when money arrives, governance home when contributors arrive. Separately, engaging the Tauri community as a contributor is a reputational asset that helps with other applications, even though it pays nothing.

## Deadline

None — rolling for Commons Conservancy programme adoption.
