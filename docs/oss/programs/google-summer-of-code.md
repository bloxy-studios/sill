# Google Summer of Code

Status: SEASONAL — 2026 organization round closed 2026-02-03 (Last verified: 2026-08-21)

## Program

Annual global program in which Google pays new contributors a stipend to work on a 12+ week project with an accepted open source mentoring organization. Running since 2005; 1,000+ organizations and 21,000+ contributors to date. Around 30 new organizations are admitted each year.

## Official URL

https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/ (2026 org call: https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/01/mentor-org-applications-for-google-summer-of-code-2026-open-through-feb-3.html)

## What it funds

Contributor stipends, paid by Google to the contributor. The mentoring organization receives no money. This is a talent-pipeline and mentoring program, not project funding — a common and expensive misconception when planning a funding roadmap.

## Eligibility

Mentoring organization requirements, from Google's official 2026 call:

1. An established open source project with at least 18 months of history.
2. Software released under an OSI-approved license.
3. A robust community with members prepared to mentor (the mentor guide specifies at least 5 active people; the FAQ requires at least 2 mentors).
4. An active project with regular engagement, not infrequent contributions.
5. A comprehensive project ideas list, scoped to 90/175/350-hour project sizes.
6. Demonstrated understanding of GSoC's goals and rules.
7. A high-quality, fully answered application.
   Competitive: 450+ organizations apply annually, roughly 150-200 accepted, so more than half are rejected. Google stated an expanded focus on AI, Security and Machine Learning domains for 2026.

## Typical funding

$0 to the organization. Contributor stipends are set and paid by Google; amounts vary by region and project size.

## Application requirements

Organization application via the GSoC site during the window, including an ideas list (the single most important element), organization profile, licence, and answers on mentor engagement and contributor retention. Incomplete applications are auto-rejected; there are no deadline extensions.

## Sill fit

NOT ELIGIBLE for at least 18 months, and even then this is a labour-and-community program, not funding. Sill currently fails requirements 1, 3 and 4 outright: no history, no community, no mentors besides the maintainer. Worth being blunt: a solo maintainer with no contributors would also struggle to mentor effectively, and Google explicitly evaluates retention of past contributors.

## Missing evidence

- 18+ months of project history from first public commit.
- At least 5 active community members and 2 people willing and able to mentor.
- Regular commit activity and visible community engagement (issues, discussions, reviews).
- A written ideas list with 90/175/350-hour scoped projects.

## Application strategy

Not a funding path — reclassify it internally as a contributor-recruitment path and revisit only once Sill has a real contributor community. If pursued eventually, the ideas list must be written months before the January window. Google Season of Docs, the sibling program, has been terminated.
